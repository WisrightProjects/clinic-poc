Demo of ClinicAI — Patient Intake & Voice Transcription

ClinicAI has two parts. An attender uses the Android app to register each patient and record their spoken answers; the app transcribes them and writes an AI summary. A doctor then reviews that summary and the full Q&A on the web dashboard. Work through the six steps below in order — they follow one patient from the front desk to the doctor. Tick each one off as you try it.

Stage Proof of concept Attender app Android Doctor dashboard Web browser Languages Tamil & English Takes about 15 minutes

Before you begin

This is a proof of concept, not the finished product. It is built to show the whole journey working end to end — register a patient, record answers by voice, transcribe them, summarise, and review. Some parts run on stand-ins for now; those are marked clearly in each step and gathered again at the end.

The two parts talk to the same backend. A patient you register in the app appears in the doctor's queue on the web, and sending an intake to the doctor is what makes it show up for review. Keep both open side by side to see this.

Speech-to-text and the AI summary are the two "AI" pieces. Transcription needs its small language service running behind the scenes. The written summary is live — a paid AI engine (Kimi) reads the patient's real answers and writes a genuine summary, sending the answers to an external provider to do so. Both are explained in Step 3 and Step 4, and again at the end.

Anything with an open tick box is ready for you to try now. Anything marked Stand-in, Needs service or Not yet has a greyed-out box that cannot be ticked — please read it before you try that part of the screen.

1

Each department has its own short list of intake questions. Open the gear icon (⚙) at the top right of the home screen to see and edit the active template before you start taking patients.

2

From Home, tap to add a new patient. Fill in their details and start the visit. Behind the scenes the patient gets a token number for the day — you will see it on the queue in step 5. Tokens restart at 1 each day, so the same numbers reappear tomorrow; that is intended.

3

The intake screen lists every question with a progress bar (for example 2 / 5). Tap a question to open the recorder, capture the patient's spoken answer, and save it. The app uploads the audio and writes back the transcript.

  • Needs serviceTranscription (speech-to-text) runs on a small free language service that must be switched on behind the scenes. When it is running, spoken Tamil or English is turned into text automatically. If it is not running, the answer still saves safely and is shown as transcription failed — nothing is lost and the app does not crash. A paid engine (Sarvam) can be used instead for higher accuracy.
4

Once every question is answered, review the whole intake on one screen — each question with its transcript, plus an AI-generated summary — then send it to the doctor. Sending is what makes the patient appear in the doctor's queue for review.

  • Live AI · paidThe summary is generated by a real AI engine (Kimi) from the actual Q&A — it is different for every patient, not a fixed sample. This is a paid service and it sends the patient's answers to an external AI provider to write the summary.
5

Home is the attender's landing screen — today's patients in a list, with each one's stage shown by the colour of its token. The count at the top is today's queue, not an all-time total.

3Grey — completed 4Blue — in progress now 5Yellow — waiting / up next
6

Open the doctor dashboard in a web browser. The patients you sent in the app appear here in today's queue, using the same token colours. Pick a patient to read their summary and full Q&A, then mark them done.

  • Not yet"Currently with the doctor" is not shown. The dashboard tracks waiting → in progress → done, but there is no separate "being seen right now" stage yet — a doctor picking a patient on screen is not recorded. We can add this if it is wanted.
  • Stand-inSign-in and roles. There is no real login yet — the app is fixed as the attender and the dashboard as the doctor. Proper accounts and permissions come with the production build.

Running on stand-ins · for the production build

What is a stand-in right now

These pieces are wired through the whole journey so you can see the flow end to end, but they run on a placeholder for the demo. Each is a known, planned swap for the production build. Tap a row to read where it stands.

  • The summary shown to the attender and doctor is written live by the real AI engine (Kimi) from the patient's actual answers, so it is different for every patient. It is a paid service and sends the answers to an external AI provider.
  • Transcription runs on a small free language service (Whisper) that has to be switched on behind the scenes. With it running, spoken Tamil and English become text automatically. With it off, answers still save and are marked "transcription failed" so nothing is lost. A paid engine (Sarvam) is the higher-accuracy alternative.
  • No real login yet. The app is fixed as "attender" and the dashboard as "doctor". Real accounts, passwords and permissions are part of the production build.
  • There are no push notifications in this build. The attender and doctor see new items by opening or refreshing their screen. Alerts can be added later.
  • The attender app is Android-first for the proof of concept. An iPhone build follows once an Apple developer account and sign-off are in place. The doctor dashboard is a website and already works on any browser.

New in this build

  • Token colours by stage — grey for completed, blue for in progress, yellow for waiting — in both the app and the dashboard
  • The app's colours and buttons now match the web dashboard, so the two feel like one product
  • The queue count is now scoped to today's patients, not the all-time total
  • Each queue row shows a two-line summary preview and a 2 / 5 progress figure beside the name
  • The doctor's Seen today / Remaining / Q&A stats, and a corrected visit date, so the day's figures are right
